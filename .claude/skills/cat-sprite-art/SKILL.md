# Cat sprite art

Cats are SVG generated by the **Cats** section of `tools/ui/make_svgs.py` (`CATS`, `CAT_HEAD`,
`CAT_BODY`, `cat_svg`). Output: `game/ui/skin/cat_<kind>.svg` (full body, 256×240) and
`cat_head_<kind>.svg` (head only, for avatars). Kinds now: `cream tabby grey calico hero`.
Used by `Ui.platform("cat_…")` and the HUD avatar in `game/ui/Ui.gd`.

**The hero (defender) cat must read as "the hero" at a glance, even as a 60 px avatar.** Its
signature, which no regular cat may reuse: silver knight helmet with built-in metal cat ears,
gold band and a red plume (the silhouette); ginger coat with a white muzzle and chest (the
colour); main sword slung across the back with the hilt above the left shoulder; blue cape
with a gold hem and shoulder clasps (hero colours blue + gold); determined brows. Regular cats
keep plain faces and no headgear unless they are wearing an outfit.

**This is a different style from the UI skin** (see the `roguelike-idle-ui` skill): no
two-tone faces, no gloss, no drop shadow. Don't mix the two.

## Style rules (measured from the "Asset bổ sung" Figma page)

- **Flat fills only.** No shading, highlight or gradient anywhere on the cat.
- **Character line:** `#583f21`, round caps and joins. Legacy source uses an even width
  ≈ 5% of sprite height (12 px at 240 px); new proposals use the accepted 9 px main line.
  Equipment interior ink follows the current proposal contract below rather than this legacy color treatment.
- **Head:** a wide mochi/bun shape, a bit wider than the body, overlapping the body's top.
- **Ears:** triangles with rounded tips, pink inside (`#f6b8b0`), drawn *before* the head so
  the head covers their base.
- **Body:** chubby, about as wide as the head, slightly pear-shaped.
- **Paws:** two short, thick **hook strokes held at the chest**, mirrored — the left one goes
  down then curls outward like a "J", the right one is its mirror. They sit close to the
  centre line, just below the head. *Not* two vertical brackets ")(" — that reads as a fold.
- **Feet:** two round bumps on the bottom edge with the outline notched up between them.
  *No* line drawn inside the body.
- **Tail:** a curled stroke behind the body (dark stroke under a colour stroke).
- **Face:** two solid dot eyes (`#3b2a1a`) and an "ω" mouth; no blush, no nose highlight.
- **Coat colours:** cream `#f2d2ac`, tabby `#f9d27b` + stripes `#d89f4c`, grey `#c4c4c4` +
  stripes `#8a8a8a`, white `#fdfaf5` with patches (calico `#f2a65a` / `#4a3a32`). Stripes and
  patches are clipped to the head/body shape.
- **Outfits** (jacket, police uniform, sweater in the references) sit over the body as their own
  flat shapes with the character's exterior line; paws stay on top of the clothes. Use the
  current equipment contract below for interior construction lines in new proposals.

Draw order: tail → cape (if any) → body → stripes/patches → paws → scarf/clothes → ears (outer,
then pink) → head fill → head marks → head outline → face → hat/crown.

## Workflow

1. **Get enough reference.** One small sprite is not enough — the first attempt at the paws
   was wrong because only one 147 px cat was checked. Figma file `wMtwWaYIiKUchUdq5YdTm5`,
   page `3470:2`. Sitting cats (211–214 × 230 px): `3471:252` (cream), `3471:356` (grey,
   sweater), `3471:492` (blue, police), `3471:914` (tabby, red jacket), `3471:1176`,
   `3471:1401` (white, blue hoodie), `3471:1549`, `3471:1725`; loaf `3471:215`; split parts
   `3471:258`, `3471:313` (head, body, ears, paws, tail drawn separately for animation).
   Fetch with the claude.ai Figma MCP `get_screenshot` (`contentsOnly: true,
   enableBase64Response: true`); the PNG is saved in the session's `tool-results/`.
2. **Zoom the part you are unsure about.** Crop and upscale ×4 with PIL, or print the area as
   ASCII (`#` = line pixels) to see which way a stroke curls.
3. **Edit `make_svgs.py`** (never the generated SVG), then run the pipeline:
   ```powershell
   python -I tools/ui/make_svgs.py
   & $godot --headless --path . --import
   & $godot --headless --path . --script res://tools/ui/preview_skin.gd -- C:\tmp\cats.png cat_
   ```
   `$godot = "C:\Users\dangp\OneDrive\Desktop\Godot_v4.7.2-stable_win64.exe"`.
4. **Compare side by side** at the same height — reference first, then yours:
   `python -I .claude/skills/roguelike-idle-ui/scripts/compare.py out.png ref.png C:\tmp\cats.png --height 320`
   (crop single cats out of the 180 px-cell preview sheet first). Look for: paw shape and
   height, body width vs head, feet notch, eye spacing, line weight.
5. In context: `tests/ui/LobbyPreview.tscn` shows four cats on stands and the hero head in the
   HUD avatar (`.claude/skills/roguelike-idle-ui/scripts/shot.ps1` renders it).

## Adding things

- **New coat:** add an entry to `CATS` (`fill`, stripe colour or `(patch1, patch2)`); add a
  branch in `cat_svg` only if it needs new marks.
- **New outfit/accessory:** a `kind`-specific block in `cat_svg`, inserted at the right point
  in the draw order; keep it flat with the 12 px line (7–10 px for small trims).
- **Poses (lying, attacking, hit):** keep the same head and line, change the body path and paw
  strokes; the references split cats into head/body/ears/paws/tail so parts can be animated
  separately — follow that if animation is needed.

## Known gaps

The references are hand-drawn: their outline wobbles slightly and the ears flow into the head
outline; ours are cleaner geometric curves. Reference faces have the eyes a little further
apart and a tiny nose joined to the mouth. Say so when comparing.

## Unit sprites (soldiers, generals, monsters)

`tools/ui/make_units.py` (run after make_svgs.py) writes `game/ui/skin/unit_<id>.svg`, 256×240,
weapon baked in; troops use `unit_key = "art:<id>"` and SpriteLib bakes them to 128×120.
- **Cat soldiers** (16, sheet "Solider (sửa)"): coat by class (Bộ binh tabby, Tầm xa grey,
  Pháp sư calico, Kỵ binh cream); outfit + headgear by class (kettle hat + breastplate · green
  hood with ear points + quiver strap · wizard hat + robe · bronze crest helmet + red tabard);
  trim colour = rarity (leather, blue, purple, gold); Huyền thoại adds a red cape with gold hem.
  The weapon icon sits at the right paw; the right paw stroke is drawn again on top so it holds it.
- **Generals** (`unit_hero_<id>`, Heroes.gd): same outfits, always caped, small gold crown.
- **Monsters** (the enemies, dungeon theme): slime, goblin (sword, bow, hammer + apron, bomb),
  skeleton (lance, greatsword + round shield, crossbow with fire eyes), bat, mole miner, mushroom
  healer, orc cook (chef hat), Vua Orc (crown + cape). Grumpy brows + one tooth = enemy face.
- Same line and flat-fill rules as the cats. Never use the hero cat's knight helmet elsewhere.
- **Cá (fish)**, the in-battle resource icon (`icon_fish*`, `fish_flat()` in make_svgs.py), follows these cat rules too, not the UI icon style: flat blue body, pale belly, forked tail, #583f21 line ~5% of the size (2.5 px min), dot eye. Keep any food/creature icon in this style.

## Weapons and equipment: cat style too (user rule)

Anything a cat holds or wears — the 16 weapons (`icon_w_*`), hero equipment (`icon_eq_*`), the
fish — is drawn in this flat style, **not** the glossy UI icon style. In make_svgs.py the parts
are still built with the `Icon` class, but rendered with `Icon.flat_svg(size)`: each part one
flat colour (40% between its light and dull tones) with its own #583f21 outline (~5% of the
size, 2.5 px min), open strokes as a dark stroke under a colour stroke, later parts covering
earlier outlines, and every `gloss` raw (shine, blade light, sparkles, anything with opacity)
dropped. Keep real details (wraps, rivets, runes, gems, hamon). make_units.py embeds the same
flat drawing in the units' hands with `flat_svg(128, line=10/scale)`, so the held weapon carries a line as heavy as the cat's 12 px (an icon-weight line looks thin next to the cat).

For new proposals, keep the user's accepted 9 px main-line target on the 256 x 240 canvas for cats and weapons, superseding legacy generator widths. After rejecting the equipment redraw's interior strokes and re-studying the reference, the user rated the new spear sample 9/10. Use [the accepted spear](../../../tools/skills/casual-weapon-design/references/accepted-spear.md) as the visual baseline; its straight shaft measures approximately 8.2 px after fitting, not exactly 9 px everywhere. Do not automatically make every component boundary 9 px or every interior gray. Distinguish silhouette edges (including holes), actual overlaps, surface seams/ornament and filled material planes. Surface ink responds to the material; secondary weights require review at fitted size. Follow [the re-studied stroke reference](../../../tools/skills/casual-weapon-design/references/internal-strokes.md) for observed examples and current decisions. Do not turn facets into lines, and do not infer that reference shading authorizes shading on the flat cats. Earlier hero-equipment prototypes remain unapproved; the accepted spear is a review asset and has not been integrated into production.
